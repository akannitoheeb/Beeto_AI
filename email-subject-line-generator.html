      How to write a subject line people open

      The subject line has one job: get the right person to open the email. It competes with dozens of other messages, so it needs to be clear before it is clever.

      • Say what is inside. A reader should know why to open it. Vague teasers can win one open and lose trust.
      • Put the important words first. Phones cut subject lines off at roughly 40 characters, and it varies by device and app.
      • Keep it short. Around 30 to 50 characters works for most emails. Read it aloud. If it needs a breath, trim it.
      • Be specific. A number, a name or a real detail beats a general claim.
      • Match the email. If the subject promises something the email does not deliver, people unsubscribe or mark it as spam.
      • Test, then keep what works. Send two versions to part of your list and use the better one for the rest.

      What can hurt delivery

      Words like "free", "urgent" and "act now", all-caps words, and several exclamation marks are common spam signals. They will not always send an email to spam, because filters also look at your sender reputation, authentication (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) and how recipients react. Still, there is rarely a reason to risk it.

      How long should an email subject line be?

      A good rule of thumb is 30 to 50 characters. Many phones cut subject lines off at around 40 characters, though it varies by device and email app, so put the important words first.

      Does this tool use AI?

      What are spam trigger words?

      They are words and styles often linked to junk mail, such as free, urgent, act now, all-caps words and several exclamation marks. Modern spam filters also weigh sender reputation and authentication, so a subject line alone rarely decides where an email lands.

      Should I use emojis in subject lines?

      Sparingly. One emoji can help a message stand out for some audiences, but several can look spammy. Test it with your own list instead of assuming.
